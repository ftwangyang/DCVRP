# Supplied-code audit and experiment isolation

The files in the repository root were treated as read-only.  All repairs,
tests, checkpoints, raw observations, tables, and figures produced for the
review are isolated under `experiments/`.

## Execution blockers found in the supplied implementation

1. The vehicle-selection action was a hard `argmax`, and its log-probability
   was absent from the REINFORCE objective.  A direct backward-pass diagnostic
   found no gradient on any of the 64 selector parameter tensors.
2. `train.py` did not pass the configured vehicle count to the learner, which
   retained the default of four selectors and failed for the manuscript's
   larger fleets.
3. Data normalization mapped disclosure and service times to a horizon of 1.0,
   while the environment retained a default horizon of 480.  Dynamic requests
   therefore did not enter the intended normalized planning intervals.
4. The environment arguments in the supplied training call were displaced:
   the customer mask was passed as the pending penalty and the pending penalty
   as the interval count.
5. The supplied training smoke test reached its first epoch but failed while
   exporting statistics because a tensor was written through a scalar-only
   code path.
6. At interval boundaries, future route events were removed without computing
   the vehicle's en-route position or preserving an in-progress service.  The
   original `served` flag was also set when a customer was selected, rather
   than when physical service was completed.

## Isolated compatibility behavior

- Vehicle actions are sampled during training and evaluated using the selected
  aggregation rule; their log-probabilities are included in the same policy
  gradient as customer actions.
- The configured fleet size, ten planning intervals, and normalized horizon
  are explicit in every `StudyConfig` and checkpoint.
- A boundary transition retains completed work, keeps an in-service vehicle
  unavailable until service completion, interpolates an en-route vehicle to
  its physical boundary position, and releases only unexecuted assignments.
- Executed distance is prorated at a boundary and unexecuted distance is
  refunded from the episodic objective.
- `committed`, `completed`, `late`, and `unserved` are tracked separately.
  Evaluation QoS counts only customers physically completed by the horizon.
- Evaluation cost is consistent for neural and classical methods:
  `executed distance + 5 * unserved`.

## Verification

`python -m unittest discover -s experiments/tests -v` checks:

- exact dynamic ratios per generated instance;
- expected early/late arrival-profile ordering;
- non-zero policy gradient on the independent selector;
- equality of the episodic objective and the reported executed-distance plus
  unserved-customer penalty;
- continuous en-route position and distance at a boundary; and
- committed-to-completed transition for an in-service customer.

The reviewer-study results should not be mixed with values generated by the
supplied root scripts.  The corrected definitions change what QoS and executed
distance mean, even though the manuscript method itself is kept intact.
